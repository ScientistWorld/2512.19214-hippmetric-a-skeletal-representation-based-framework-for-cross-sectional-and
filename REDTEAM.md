# Scoring audit

**Honest improvements can score higher, but this gym does not reliably reward better research on the paper's problem.** Train-selected thickness smoothing improves both hidden thickness errors, and the weaker boundary method loses on every error metric. However, removing the entire nonlinear deformation loop is a **shortcut that ties** the reference exactly. The substitute data do not establish the paper's anatomical registration effect: small-scale enumeration recovers exact medial correspondence, and the longitudinal target contains a generator-defined displacement absent from the observed anatomy. A method returning zero thickness can also beat the reference by copying the input surface. These failures matter before any shortcut filtering by a separate grader.

All measurements below come from the unchanged, no-argument `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh`. Errors are in millimeters; smaller is better. Validity is higher-is-better. The reference is the reproduced `hippmetric` in the original hidden score file, independently rerun below, rather than the partial paper numbers in `scoring/reference.json`.

The honest variant applies Gaussian regularization to the estimated thickness field on its supplied anatomical grid. It chooses bandwidth 0.9 from {0.35, 0.6, 0.9, 1.2} using only the visible train objective MAE + 0.5 × p95, then invokes the hidden test once. It does not read labels directly. [Selection evidence](redteam/better_smoothing/train_selection.json) and every raw score are retained. The hidden MAE gain is 0.099662 and p95 gain 0.239425, both greater than the observed seed spread. Because no scored metric worsens, this improvement and the weaker method's loss have the correct sign for any positive metric normalization. Removing the purported nonlinear core does not lower the score at all.

\* **Δ is an explicitly reproducible audit-cohort calculation, not a prediction of an unknown future leaderboard.** For experiment e and metric m, it sums `weight[e] × abs(coefficient[e,m]) × direction-adjusted(value − reference) / spread[e,m]`. It uses population standard deviation across the successful audit attempts listed in [rank_summary.json](redteam/rank_summary.json), counting one reference seed and excluding failed tests. Finite values determine each spread; zero-spread metrics contribute zero. Future attempts will change these denominators, so tradeoff results can change. Pareto improvements and exact ties do not depend on that choice. [analyze.py](redteam/analyze.py) uses the supplied experiment weights, metric directions and coefficient magnitudes, never the negative coefficient signs as another reversal. NaN handling is not specified by the available ranking contract: its displayed diagnostic is conditional, as explained below. No external rank implementation was available to run.

**Seed spread and reproduction check.** Full reference runs with NumPy seeds **13, 37 and 91**, on the same fixed benchmark, produce identical hidden values for every scored metric: maximum minus minimum is **0.0** everywhere, and normalized score spread is **0.0**. The original precomputed outputs also match. This method has no stochastic training, so this is determinism under method seeds, not an estimate of variability across new anatomical samples or regenerated datasets. See [seed_spread.json](redteam/seed_spread.json) and the three seed folders. Positive smoothing gains exceed this measured spread.

The packaged reference beats the packaged boundary baseline in the paper-reported direction for thickness, cross-sectional correspondence, longitudinal correspondence and reconstruction. The comparison is not a reproduction of the paper's comparisons against cm-rep, ds-rep or SPHARM-PDM. The boundary baseline pairs opposite halves of a PCA-sorted point list rather than anatomical superior/inferior sides; its 6.986488 thickness MAE exceeds the 2.645867 MAE of predicting zero. It is therefore a weak scientific control even though it leaves numerical room below the reference.

The important departures from the paper are substantive, not merely reduced sample counts. They are visible in [the reference source snapshot](redteam/reference/hippmetric.py), [the data generator](data/synthetic_hippocampus.py), [the baseline](baseline/boundary_model.py), and the local [paper](paper/2512.19214.md), principally sections 3, 4.1 and 4.3–4.4:

- The paper uses FreeSurfer FS60 meshes, ten anatomical subfields, mesh cleaning and refinement, and a Voronoi/NURBS ARMM template that handles folded subfields. Here five names designate consecutive bins along one procedural ribbon, with 341 skeletal points and 682 observed vertices. There are no clinical segmentations, folded subfield hierarchy or corresponding surface-processing pipeline. The paper's template has 549 skeletal points and 1,098 reported thickness measurements.
- The paper transports internal landmarks through Deformetrica dense-mode LDDMM. Here alignment is PCA/similarity fitting with normal-aware Hungarian assignments, followed by Gaussian kernel ridge displacement fits. There is no LDDMM flow or invertibility constraint. More decisively, `query_step_scale=0.0` disables all nonlinear updates to the returned internal points. The completed shortcut ablation removes that loop and ties every score.
- The spoke procedure uses three nearest-vertex projection and normal-mixing passes, rather than the paper's ray/mesh intersection cases and iterative length/angular optimization. The paper specifies length steps of 0.1 mm up to 1,000 iterations and angular steps of 0.1 rad up to 50 iterations. The implementation's final projection also overwrites the immediately preceding paired-length equalization. This last observation is a source inspection, not a separately measured ablation.
- Scored landmarks are generated by an independent scale/translation and local normal-aware nearest-vertex matcher, rather than by the registered and refined skeletal representation. Follow-ups reuse the baseline skeleton without the paper's baseline/follow-up surface registration. The outputs measured by different experiments need not describe a common anatomical model.
- Data comprise 24 synthetic subjects with four timepoints, small rigid rotations, isotropic scaling, noiseless supplied normals and exactly opposite upper/lower noise. The skeleton geometry does not deform across time. Thickness changes locally, but longitudinal labels additionally move by `0.84 × timepoint` along a radial direction without this displacement appearing in the surface. The visible generator encodes this rule. Copying that rule improves longitudinal error without better anatomical reconstruction.
- Clinical manual thickness/landmark annotation, the paper's progressively eroded anatomical follow-ups, comparisons to the actual shape-model baselines, ADNI/GHABS pathological robustness, and clinical reliability/biomarker validation are absent. In particular, this is not the paper's robustness comparison on 556 A−/CU and 271 A+/CI participants.
- The evaluator's surface HD is twice the largest bidirectional nearest-neighbor distance; the paper defines Hausdorff distance as the maximum of the two directed distances, without that factor. The evaluator's Q is an RMS distance, whereas the paper prints `sqrt(sum of squared distances) / n`. Paper-scale numbers in `reference.json` are therefore not evidence of matched numerical reproduction. Arbitrary vertex subsampling creates a further error discussed below.

Consequently, better scores here can mean improved synthetic denoising or matching the label-generation convention. The experiments do not show that they mean a better version of the paper's method or problem.

**What the score measures and misses.** The reference is not globally saturated. The only scored quantities already at their theoretical best bound are `valid_spoke_fraction=1.0` in **each** of `subfield_thickness_accuracy`, `correspondence_stability` and `surface_robustness`. Every scored error is strictly above its lower bound of zero, as the reference table shows. `surface_q_mm=1.239288` is reported but has coefficient zero and does not affect rank. The label-copy shortcuts attain all error bounds; those wins do not depend on the future normalization pool.

The scoring weights and coefficient magnitudes are:

| Experiment weight | Scored metrics and absolute coefficients |
|---|---|
| Thickness: 0.25 | MAE 1.0; p95 0.5; validity 0.4 |
| Correspondence: 0.50 | Cross mean 0.8; cross p95 0.35; longitudinal mean 1.0; longitudinal p95 0.45; validity 0.3 |
| Surface: 0.25 | Q 0.7; HD 1.0; p95 0.45; validity 0.3; q 0.0 |

All errors have `higher_is_better=false`; validity has `higher_is_better=true`. The negative error coefficients do not reverse this direction again. Normalization means the coefficient magnitudes express importance, rather than millimeter units. No compute-time metric rewards retaining the disabled nonlinear procedure.

There is **no requirement that task success precede compensation across metrics**. In the measured cohort the zero-thickness shortcut receives −0.502096 from thickness and +0.725893 from copied reconstruction, totaling **+0.223797** while retaining reference landmark scores. Its rank depends on the normalization pool, but it is a concrete, successful finite-output counterexample to the intended safeguard. Validity only checks finite/nonnegative thickness and finite coordinate arrays, capped by the method's reported number. It does not check spoke intersections, mesh attachment, thickness/endpoint consistency, topology, or whether reconstruction and correspondence belong to the same skeleton. Zero thickness passes with validity 1.

The surface test applies the same hashed vertex indices to the reconstruction and the independently shuffled observed cloud **before** nearest-neighbor matching. These indices do not identify the same spatial subset in an ordered template reconstruction. Reversing the reconstruction's rows changes its score although the geometry is unchanged; copying the observed row order gets exactly zero. This is an additional metric defect, beyond the question of whether reconstruction alone should be rewarded.

The output-coverage and failure requirements also fail. A nonempty method list from editable `scores_train.json` filters the on-disk artifacts, as the whitelist shortcut demonstrates. The two failed-output shortcuts abort the whole scorer before writing any new score file; they do not produce a worst-valued entry for every metric of every experiment. Stale or prewritten empty entries survive. Their status is explicitly unsuccessful here; the audit does not treat their stale files as successful hidden scores. The NaN shortcut instead succeeds and writes non-standard JSON numeric values, which also must not be treated as completed valid measurements. There is no evaluator-owned learned model to retrain in this gym; replacement of the purportedly fixed label artifact is the tested equivalent, and succeeds.

**Enumeration and problem scale.** The brute-force shortcut enumerates 3,751 pairs per scan (31 lamellae × 121 pairs), across all 96 scans. Noiseless normals identify lamellae; exactly anticorrelated upper/lower noise makes their centroids noise-free. A similarity fit recovers the medial grid, and pair enumeration recovers the corresponding endpoints with maximum midpoint discrepancy **1.822931e−14 mm**. Its artifact-generation subprocess takes **1.925 s**, versus **33.370 s** for the full reference seed-13 subprocess in the same job. These are indicative wall times including Python startup/I/O, not controlled performance claims or ranked metrics. [Diagnostics](redteam/shortcut_bruteforce/diagnostics.json) retain candidate count and precision.

Thus enumeration solves the synthetic underlying correspondence that registration should recover: the test is too small and structured to exercise the paper's need for nonlinear anatomical registration. It does not make every scored error zero: observed thickness still contains noise and longitudinal labels contain the artificial displacement. Its longitudinal mean and surface HD are worse than the reference, so its additive win is cohort-dependent. The exact correspondence recovery, the nonlinear-removal tie and the label-drift shortcut together are evidence against the validity of this substitute problem.

**Execution and preservation.** CPU job `21efe95e-273` completed the first 22 cases with exit 0; CPU job `96b07f39-5b9` completed the finite zero-thickness case and regenerated the score analysis with exit 0. Each candidate first produces persistent files and exits, visible train evaluation runs, then a separate subprocess runs exactly `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh` with no arguments. No producer process survives into the hidden evaluation. The job bundles cases to reuse reference outputs; a fresh replay regenerates missing reference/baseline outputs from scientist-visible code. No candidate uses hidden evaluator imports, changes evaluator files, alters Python resolution, or tampers with the evaluator process. Hidden test/source hashes remain unchanged in every [status file](redteam/reference_seed_13/status.json).

The common implementation is [candidate.py](redteam/candidate.py), installed under `method/` for each run; [harness.py](redteam/harness.py) handles installation, invocation, evidence and restoration. Each case has `run.sh`, `scores_train.json`, `scores_test.json` and `status.json`; failed tests also have `failure.txt`. Raw NaNs are intentionally preserved as evidence. [Final verification](redteam/verification.json) checks all 23 saved case records, syntax, reference ties, shortcut bounds and restoration of gym paths to the initial revision. All candidate changes outside `redteam/` are restored after each run, and the temporary job launcher is restored to the original git revision at completion. The report and audit directory are the deliverable; pre-existing infrastructure-managed README/manifest and trajectory status changes were not edited by this audit.

For the next gym iteration, the priority is an anatomically meaningful test that needs registration and validates one coherent geometric output. Evaluation must own fresh hidden inputs/targets rather than reuse visible deterministic answers or editable label files. Surface distances must be invariant to vertex ordering and compare spatially valid sets. Require actual spoke/endpoint consistency and task success before additive rewards can offset failures. Enumerate every submitted artifact independently of train result keys, reject nonfinite outputs, and atomically write explicit complete worst-valued records for failed methods. Direct JSON forgery is already overwritten correctly; preserving that behavior does not address the larger scientific failures above.
