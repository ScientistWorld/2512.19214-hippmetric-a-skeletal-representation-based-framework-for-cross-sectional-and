# Problem Definition

## Research Question

Given a 3D binary segmentation of a hippocampus (one per scan, on a 0.5 mm grid), place a fixed anatomical coordinate system into it: for every point of a fixed template sheet (61 lamellae along the long axis x 9 positions across it), report where that anatomical point lies in the scan and how far the tissue extends from it towards the superior and inferior boundary. From this, local thickness is read off at anatomically corresponding positions across subjects, and across a subject's follow-up scans while tissue is being lost.

## Why It Matters

Hippocampal atrophy is a major imaging biomarker in ageing and Alzheimer's disease, and it is regional (subfield- and long-axis-specific). Global volumes miss it. Local measurements are only comparable across people and over time if the same anatomical location is measured each time; segmentation noise and shape variability make that hard, and correspondence that drifts as tissue disappears turns real atrophy into apparent shape change.

## Data

Two cohorts, each with baseline + 3 follow-up scans per subject and its own template (both 61 x 9, u = 0 at the head):

- **Procedural cohort.** `data/hippo_template.npz` (template), `data/hippo_train.npz` (32 development subjects), `data/hippo_train_labels.npz` (their ground truth). Subjects differ in shape, size, pose and thickness pattern; about half carry baseline regional thinning; follow-ups lose tissue in a subject-specific region. Ground truth includes the true position of every template point, so cross-sectional correspondence is known.
- **Real-anatomy cohort.** Baselines are real hippocampi: manual labels from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon Task04 (Vanderbilt, CC-BY-SA 4.0; fetched by `scripts/download.sh`). `data/hippo_real_anatomy.npz` holds the 228 development labels (1 mm, whole hippocampus and head label), `data/hippo_real_template.npz` the template built from their mean shape (`data/build_real_template.py`). Follow-ups lose tissue in part of the head and also differ from the baseline by a smooth nonrigid deformation and a new pose. The development scans and their ground truth are generated by `eval/train/real_cohort.py` (`python eval/train/evaluate.py --export-real-inputs PATH` writes the scans). Only longitudinal quantities have ground truth here: where each baseline point is on the follow-ups and how much thickness it lost.
- Segmentations in both cohorts contain boundary inconsistencies, label noise, islands and holes. The input file's `cohort` entry says which cohort (and template) applies.
- The scored test cohorts (32 subjects x 4 timepoints each; the real-anatomy test subjects are held-out MSD cases with their own hidden follow-up changes) are not distributed; predictors are run on them by the hidden evaluator.

## Success Criteria

Accurate local thickness and thickness change, low point-wise correspondence error across subjects and across a subject's follow-ups (inside the atrophic region and where nothing is lost), and an implied boundary that stays on the true anatomy, while keeping the local measurement geometry valid (endpoints on the boundary, no crossing measurement directions).
